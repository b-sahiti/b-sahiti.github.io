01 · Real-time distributed systems

Byzantine-resilient power-grid protection

Protecting critical grid assets when relays and networks may be compromised—without violating the substation's quarter-cycle response requirement.

Systems challenge

A compromised protective relay can fail to isolate a dangerous electrical fault or trip unnecessarily. Either outcome can damage high-value equipment and disrupt power delivery. Traditional Byzantine-resilient systems were not designed for this strict real-time environment.

Architecture and contribution

I developed the architecture and protocols for real-time Byzantine-resilient substation protection, combining diverse replicated relays, resilient networking, proactive recovery, and continuous operation under successful component and network attacks.

System building and validation

This work became part of Spire, an open-source intrusion-tolerant SCADA system. It was delivered for deployment at PNNL and major power-grid vendors including GE, Siemens, and Hitachi Energy, and evaluated through a Sandia National Laboratories red-team exercise.

02 · Cyber-physical resilience

Compound-threat analysis and tolerance

Understanding what happens when severe physical disruption is followed by a coordinated cyberattack against already-degraded critical infrastructure.

Systems challenge

Natural disasters can damage infrastructure and communications while forcing emergency reconfiguration. A capable attacker can exploit that degraded state, turning individually survivable events into a compound threat with cascading consequences.

Research contribution

I helped define the compound-threat model and developed methods to analyze how cyberattacks interact with physical disruption across control systems, networks, and recovery mechanisms. The work exposed weaknesses in static architectures and motivated more adaptable resilient-system designs.

From analysis to resilience

The research connected data-centric impact analysis with system-level protection and recovery, informing reconfiguration capabilities in Spire and a broader approach to severe-impact-resilient control systems. It received the IEEE SRDS 2024 Best Paper Award.
